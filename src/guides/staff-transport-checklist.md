Most staff transport problems start before the first car arrives. A missed pickup at 05:30 or a dispute over the month-end invoice usually traces back to something nobody wrote down at the start. Use this checklist before you sign with any transport provider, including us.

## 1. Know who needs a ride, and when

- **Headcount per shift**, not just in total. Ten people on a night shift need a different plan from ten people on days.
- **Shift start and end times**, including weekends and public holidays.
- **Home areas.** A rough map of where people live (suburb level is enough) decides how many cars you need and how long the routes take.
- **Sites.** If staff work across more than one building, list every address.

## 2. Agree how bookings and changes work

People swap shifts, get sick and go on leave. Decide up front:

- How a staff member books, changes or cancels a trip, and by when.
- What a late cancellation costs, and whether cancelling the trip home is treated differently.
- Who at your company can see bookings and approve exceptions.

If changes run through a WhatsApp group, someone will miss a message. A booking app with a clear cut-off avoids most of the arguments.

## 3. Set the safety rules in writing

Ask your provider to confirm, in writing:

- How drivers are vetted (licence, professional driving permit, background check).
- How they confirm the right person is getting in the car, for example a personal boarding code.
- That only booked riders travel, and drop-offs are only at the booked address.
- How long a driver waits before moving on, and who contacts the rider.
- What happens on late-night trips if a rider doesn't answer.

## 4. Make sure you can see what's happening

You shouldn't have to phone a driver to find out where a car is. Look for:

- Live tracking of each trip, from pickup to drop-off.
- A record of who boarded and when.
- Lateness and no-shows recorded per trip, so problems can be fixed rather than argued about.

## 5. Decide how you'll check the invoice

The invoice should come from the trips that actually ran. Before you start, agree:

- The rate (per rider per month, per trip or per route) and what's included.
- Whether billing is in advance or in arrears.
- How trips the provider couldn't run are credited.
- Any extra charges (other drop-off addresses, waiting time) and that they're listed before they apply.

## 6. Plan the first month

- Send staff their booking invites a week before the start date.
- Run a trial week on one shift if you can.
- Book a check-in at the end of month one to look at on-time pickups, no-shows and cost per rider.
